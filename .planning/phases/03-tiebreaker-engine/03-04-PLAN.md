Export one named fixture object per D-10 case, each containing `{ conferenceGames: Game[], outcomes: Map<GameId, TeamId>, teamIds: Set<TeamId> }` scoped to only the teams relevant to that scenario (non-participating SEC teams may be omitted from `teamIds` for that fixture, or included with zero games -- either is acceptable since Plan 03-01's deriveConferenceRecords handles both).     1. `secTwoWayTie` -- 2 teams tied, resolved by head-to-head.
    2. `secThreeWayTie` -- 3 teams tied, resolved partway through the procedure (not step 1), exercising a genuine restart.
    3. `secFourWayTie` -- 4 teams tied, with at least one step producing a 2-2 split (2 teams advance together, 2 remain tied and restart as a pair).
    4. `secFiveWayTie` -- 5 teams tied, deep enough to exercise 2+ restart cycles.
    5. `secRestartVsContinueDivergence` -- constructed so that incorrectly treating a partial separation as "continue to next step" (rather than "restart from step one") would produce a DIFFERENT final #1/#2 pair than the correct restart behavior -- this is the single most important fixture in the matrix per PITFALLS.md Pitfall 1's own "warning sign" (a fixture too easy produces the same answer either way).
    6. `secPartialHeadToHeadGraph` -- 3 tied teams where not every pair played each other (e.g., A beat B, B beat C, A and C did not play), verifying evaluateHeadToHead's beatAllOthers/lostToAllOthers predicates are used correctly (not a naive sort) -- per PITFALLS.md Pitfall 5.
    7. `secZeroCommonOpponents` -- 2+ tied teams with zero conference opponents in common, verifying the engine falls through to the next step (never a NaN or a false 0/0 separation).
    8. `secNeedsUserInput` -- a tie that survives head-to-head, common-opponents, next-highest-placed-common-opponent, AND cumulative-opponent-win-pct all failing to separate, so the expected result is NeedsUserInput with `reason.code === 'needs-scores'`.
    9. `secCollectiveBucketComparison` -- THE KEY FIXTURE proving CONTEXT.md's D-13 decision for the SEC: 3 tied SEC teams whose next-highest-placed common opponent, when walking down the frozen base ordering, lands on a bucket containing 2+ raw-standings-tied opponent teams. Construct the win/loss numbers for that opponent bucket so that comparing each tied team's win percentage against the bucket COLLECTIVELY (summed wins / summed games across the whole bucket) produces a DIFFERENT winner than comparing against the bucket's members one opponent at a time -- mirroring `big12.fixtures.ts`'s `big12CollectiveBucketComparison` (Plan 03-06) in structure. The hand-derivation comment must show both the collective result and the one-at-a-time result explicitly, state that the collective result is the one the engine must produce, and note that -- unlike the Big 12's primary-source-confirmed treatment (D-05/D-06) -- applying this same collective-bucket rule to the SEC is a documented extrapolation per D-13, since the SEC's own tiebreaker procedure never states the Big 12's collective-bucket clause.
