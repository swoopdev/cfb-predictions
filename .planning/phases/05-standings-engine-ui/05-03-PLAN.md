## The tie-bucket decision (explicit, per the gap brief)

**Chosen: the standings layer consumes the engine's OUTPUT (`ChampionshipResult.seed1.order`
and `seed2.order`) as its tie definition. It does not import, re-derive, or reimplement any
tie-defining predicate.**

Rationale:

1. `seed1.order` is the engine's own authoritative sequence for the championship slot.
   `rules.defineTiedTeams` produced the pool — win percentage for SEC/Big Ten/Big 12 via
   `defineBucketTiedTeams`, the alternate-schedule-length rule for the ACC via
   `defineAccTiedTeams` — and `resolveTiedGroup` returns `[...winners, ...restResult.order]`
   (`engine.ts:159`). Reading `order` therefore gives the standings layer the engine's exact
   verdict without a second copy of any tie-defining predicate anywhere. This is strictly
   more DRY than exporting a bucket function for reuse (CLAUDE.md: standings and tiebreaker
   logic each have exactly one implementation), and it is literally what D-11/D-14 describe:
   "Phase 5 consumes Phase 6's resolved-result output and uses it to rank the standings
   table."

   **`order` is a sequence, NOT a complete tie partition.** The restart branch does not
   recurse on `rest`; it recurses on `nextTiedTeams = defineTiedTeams(baseOrdering, records,
   nextAlreadyCommitted)` (`engine.ts:133`) — a fully redefined pool. Any team in `rest` that
   the redefinition does not re-select never appears in `order` at all. For SEC/Big Ten/Big 12
   this is inert: `defineBucketTiedTeams` walks `baseOrdering` and returns the first bucket
   with uncommitted members, which after committing a strict subset of that same bucket is
   exactly `pool \ winners`. For the ACC it is not inert: `defineAccTiedTeams` re-anchors
   `bestGamesPlayed` on a new `bestPctGroup[0]` (`acc.ts:53-89`), so a team pulled in against
   the old anchor can fail against the new one and be dropped from `order` entirely.

   Two identical-record teams can also be split *by a step*, not just by the redefinition:
   `evaluateHeadToHead`'s round-robin branch partitions on win percentage computed only over
   games among the tied teams (`steps.ts:47-76`), and two teams with identical conference
   records routinely have different head-to-head results.
2. It satisfies the CR-01 regression invariant **by construction**, not by coincidence: the
   whole of `seed1.order` lands in a single rank component whose members are emitted in
   `order` sequence at the very top of the table, so the indices of `seed1.order` within the
   rows are 0, 1, 2, ... and the resolved champion is always row 0.
3. It preserves D-04 — but only because the rank grouping explicitly closes over conference
   record equality as well as seed-group membership. Because `order` is a sequence and not a
   partition (see rationale 1), grouping on seed membership *alone* would rank a placed team
   and its identical-record twin differently. Worked ACC counterexample: records A 3-1 (4
   conference games, .750), C 2-1 (3 games, .667), C-prime 2-1 (3 games, .667), D 5-2 (7
   games, .714). The initial pool is {A, C, C-prime} (C and C-prime pulled in on the matching
   single loss; D excluded — 7 games, neither 3 wins nor 1 loss). A step places {A, C} ahead
   of {C-prime}; the redefinition with A and C committed re-anchors on D (.714, 7 games,
   `bestWins` 5, `bestLosses` 2), which does not re-select C-prime, so `seed1.order` is
   [A, C, D] and C-prime is gone. Grouping on seed membership alone would give C rank 1 and
   C-prime some later rank — identical conference records, different ranks, a direct D-04
   violation introduced by the fix, in the exact conference that produced every reproduced
   CR-01 mismatch.

   **Therefore the rank grouping is the equivalence closure of two relations: "same seed
   group" and "identical conference wins and losses."** That restores D-04 in full while
   leaving the engine's sequence untouched, and it is robust against arbitrary Phase 6 input
   (a hand-built `ChampionshipResult` can place identical-record teams in different seed
   groups, which `resolveConferenceChampionship` itself will not produce but Phase 6 may).
4. It has the smallest blast radius of the candidate designs. Exactly one existing test
   encodes the old semantics and needs rewriting — `computeStandings.test.ts:220`. The other
   seven tests in the `ranking (STAND-04, D-04)` block (`:178, :183, :191, :196, :215, :237,
   :251`) keep their assertions verbatim, as does `feeds straight back into computeStandings`
   at `:348`.

**Rejected — reimplementing the ACC rule in the standings layer.** Forbidden by CLAUDE.md's
DRY constraint and is the exact drift that produced CR-01.

**Rejected — exporting the engine's `defineTiedTeams` and iterating it over the whole
conference to partition every team.** It produces a second grouping authority, and applying
the pull-in rule below the championship slots is an extrapolation the ACC's published policy
does not make ("no other teams may be defined as Tied Teams"). It also changes the bottom of
every table: `winPctSafe` gives a 0-0 team and a 0-2 team the same win percentage, so every
winless team in a conference would share one rank from week 1 onward — a real display
regression, and one the current test at `computeStandings.test.ts:196` was written to
prevent.

**Consequence, stated plainly:** teams the engine did *not* place, and that do not share a
conference record with a placed team, keep the existing record convention (win percentage,
then wins, then losses, then school, then id) for both their order and their rank grouping.
Two grouping rules coexist — the engine's above, the display convention below — because the
engine only ever opines on the two championship slots and has no view on 5th versus 6th
place. `needsUserInput` seeds contribute no group (D-10: Phase 5 shows no pending state),
which keeps today's fallback behaviour for unresolved ties intact.

**Second consequence, accepted deliberately:** an unplaced team that shares an exact
conference record with a placed team is pulled up next to it and inherits its rank, ahead of
unplaced teams with better records. That is D-04 winning over the record convention, and it
is the correct trade: showing two 2-1 teams at different ranks in the same table is a defect
users can see, whereas the hoist is invisible unless you are comparing against a team the
engine deliberately excluded.
